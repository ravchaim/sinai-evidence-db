## Verified New Items

### TITLE: AI Word-Frequency Analysis Statistically Distinguishes Priestly, Deuteronomistic, and Deuteronomy Scribal Strands
**CATEGORY:** literary

**EXPLANATION:** A multidisciplinary team (archaeologists, biblical scholars, physicists, mathematicians, and computer scientists from Tel Aviv University, Collège de France, the University of Haifa, Duke University, Reichman University, and the Protestant Faculty of Theology of Paris) built an interpretable statistical model that analyzes word-frequency deviations to distinguish authorial/redactional "fingerprints" across the first nine books of the Hebrew Bible (Genesis–Kings). The model was trained to separate three traditionally posited strands — Deuteronomy (D), the Deuteronomistic History running from Joshua through Kings (DtrH), and the Priestly source/material in the Torah (P) — and found that D and DtrH cluster much more closely with each other than either does with P, a result the authors say aligns with existing expert (source-critical) assessments; the method was also used to propose statistical attributions for some disputed chapters. This is directly relevant to the project's literary-unity claims (e.g., existing entries on "Stylometric Unity," "Vocabulary Drift Analysis," and "Lack of Redaction Seams," which argue the Torah shows more stylistic unity than a multi-source/late-redaction model would predict): this new, independently verified, peer-reviewed study reaches the opposite conclusion for the corpus it examines, offering a transparent statistical method that reproduces — rather than overturns — the classical source-critical (documentary-hypothesis-style) division of these books. It should be presented as a genuine, mainstream, peer-reviewed academic finding that complicates rather than supports a single-author/single-source reading of these texts, not folded into an existing "unity" entry.

**SOURCE:** Shira Faigenbaum-Golovin, Alon Kipnis, Axel Bühler, Eli Piasetzky, Thomas Römer, and Israel Finkelstein, "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship," *PLOS ONE* 20(6): e0322905 (June 3, 2025). PMID: 40460169.

**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0322905

### TITLE: A Peer-Reviewed Challenge to the Hittite-Treaty Model for Deuteronomy's Covenant Structure
**CATEGORY:** literary

**EXPLANATION:** This 2025 article in the *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* (a peer-reviewed, theologically conservative academic journal) directly challenges the long-standing scholarly argument — already used in this database (e.g., "Hittite Suzerain-Vassal Treaty Structure (Devarim)," "Hittite Treaty Formulary in Devarim," and "The Vassal Treaties of Esarhaddon and Deuteronomy's Covenant Structure") — that Deuteronomy's literary structure mirrors second-millennium-BCE Hittite (or, in the Esarhaddon-based variant, first-millennium Neo-Assyrian) suzerain-vassal treaty forms, an argument often invoked to support an earlier date for Deuteronomy's composition. Comparing Esarhaddon's Succession Treaty (a Neo-Assyrian text) against Deuteronomy 28:20–44 using explicit comparison criteria, the author reports that of seven proposed curse-parallels between the texts, only one (EST §475 // Deuteronomy 28:20) has a concrete, lexically anchored point of correspondence, and argues that even this can sustain a claim of literary dependence only by presupposing it; the article concludes the broader treaty-structure comparison is maintained in the literature only through imprecise summarization and selective exclusion of counter-evidence, and argues it should be abandoned or substantially qualified. This is a direct, peer-reviewed academic critique of an argument this database currently presents in three separate entries, and should be flagged as a live, contested scholarly debate rather than settled support — it comes from a scholar within a generally traditionalist journal, not a "minimalist" or hostile source, which makes it harder to dismiss as ideologically motivated.

**SOURCE:** Mark Steven Francois, "Is It Time to Abandon the Hittite Treaty Structure of Deuteronomy?," *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 68.4 (2025): 705–726.

**URL:** https://etsjets.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/JETS_68.4_705_Francois.pdf
