## New item

TITLE: The Qumran Sect's 364-Day Calendar — A Historically Failed Alternative to the Torah's Lunisolar System
CATEGORY: history_corrob
EXPLANATION: A study by Prof. Eshbal Ratzon of Tel Aviv University's Department of Jewish Philosophy and the Cohn Institute for the History and Philosophy of Science and Ideas, published in the peer-reviewed journal *Tarbiz: A Quarterly for Jewish Studies*, proposes a solution to a decades-old scholarly puzzle: whether the Qumran sect's distinctive 364-day calendar (attested in Dead Sea Scrolls texts such as the calendrical documents and echoed in works like Jubilees) was ever used in actual practice, or was merely a theoretical/literary construct. Ratzon argues it was genuinely practiced in the sect's early, formative years — and that the calendar dispute was itself a central driver of the sect's break from the mainstream Jerusalem religious establishment, which followed the Torah-mandated lunisolar calendar (months fixed by the new moon, corrected periodically against the solar year so that Aviv/Passover stays in spring, per Devarim 16:1). Because a 364-day year runs about a day and a quarter short of the true solar year, the sect's calendar drifted increasingly out of sync with the seasons — by roughly four weeks after twenty years if uncorrected — a problem Ratzon argues became untenable over time. Combined with improved political relations between the sect and the Hasmonean court under Alexander Jannaeus (who favored a halachic outlook closer to the sect's own and opposed the Pharisaic leadership), this practical failure led the sect to eventually abandon its rigid solar calendar and adopt the more workable lunisolar calendar used at the Jerusalem Temple. For the project's themes, this is a piece of scholarly history_corrob: it is independent academic corroboration, anchored to a specific historical Hasmonean-era political context, of the real, documented sectarian calendar controversies among Second Temple Jews reflected in the Qumran corpus — and it specifically documents the practical failure of a rival, non-Torah-mandated calendar system, in contrast to the lunisolar system the Torah itself prescribes. The study's own conclusions are about Qumran sectarian history and do not themselves argue for the Torah's calendar being validated by this; that inference is the project's own, and is presented here as such rather than as the study's claim.
SOURCE: Eshbal Ratzon, study published in *Tarbiz: A Quarterly for Jewish Studies* (Hebrew University of Jerusalem), September 2026; reported independently with consistent researcher, department, and journal attribution by Tel Aviv University's own press release, The Jerusalem Post, The Times of Israel, ScienceDaily, EurekAlert!, JNS, and Popular Archaeology.
URL: https://www.jpost.com/archaeology/article-902571
