TITLE: Langlois's Reassessment of the Tel Dan Stele Fragment Assembly
CATEGORY: archaeology
EXPLANATION: In "The Tel Dan Inscription after 30 Years," published in the Israel Exploration Journal (vol. 74, no. 2, 2024), epigrapher Michael Langlois (University of Strasbourg) used Reflectance Transformation Imaging to carry out a fresh letter-by-letter comparative analysis of the three inscribed fragments (A, B1, B2) of the 9th-century BCE Aramaic Tel Dan Stele — the artifact whose fragment A contains the phrase "House of David" (bytdwd), already covered by this project's "Tel Dan Stele" entry. Langlois found that the script style is essentially identical between fragments B1 and B2, but noticeably different from fragment A, indicating the pieces were likely mis-assembled in the stele's current museum reconstruction: A and B probably belong to separated (rather than side-by-side) portions of the original monument, or possibly even to two related but physically distinct steles. Multiple independent reports on this study are explicit that the reassessment concerns only the physical reconstruction of the fragments, not the reading of the "House of David" phrase itself, which sits squarely within fragment A and is unaffected. The value for this project is as an honest methodological footnote to the existing Tel Dan entry: even a monument central to the "House of David" evidentiary claim is subject to normal scholarly correction and refinement over time, and the correction here narrows rather than undermines the artifact's evidentiary force.
SOURCE: Michael Langlois, "The Tel Dan Inscription after 30 Years," Israel Exploration Journal 74, no. 2 (2024): 59–79.
URL: https://michaellanglois.org/publications/linscription-de-tel-dan-30-ans-plus-tard/

TITLE: Computational Word-Frequency Study Distinguishes D, Deuteronomistic, and Priestly Authorial Voices
CATEGORY: literary
EXPLANATION: Faigenbaum-Golovin, Kipnis, Bühler, Piasetzky, Römer, and Finkelstein published "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship" in PLOS ONE 20(6): e0322905 (2025; preprint on arXiv, 2410.19883). The team applied a statistical method sensitive to deviations in word frequency to 50 chapters across the Enneateuch (Genesis–Kings), sorted into three corpora identified by mainstream source-critical scholarship: the older layer of Deuteronomy (D), the Deuteronomistic History in Joshua–Kings (DtrH), and the Priestly source (P). The analysis found statistically significant, automatically detectable linguistic differences between these corpora — with D and DtrH found to be much more closely related to each other than either is to P, a result the authors say matches existing expert source-critical assessments. This is a genuine, peer-reviewed finding that should be reported honestly rather than omitted: it is direct computational evidence for the kind of composite, multi-author history behind the Pentateuch/Former Prophets that source criticism (the Documentary Hypothesis and its descendants) has long argued for, and it stands in real tension with this project's entries defending single/unified authorship (e.g., the Cassuto literary-unity argument already in the database). The dating, sourcing, and division of the Torah's component strands remains a genuinely contested question in the field, and this study is best read as new statistical support for the mainstream critical (multi-source) position rather than as evidence for the project's own thesis.
SOURCE: S. Faigenbaum-Golovin, A. Kipnis, A. Bühler, E. Piasetzky, T. Römer, I. Finkelstein, "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship," PLOS ONE 20, no. 6 (2025): e0322905.
URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0322905
