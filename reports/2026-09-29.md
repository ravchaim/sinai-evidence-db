## New Item

**TITLE:** The Ophel "House of Mikvehs" — A Monumental Second Temple Purification Complex at the Foot of the Temple Mount

**CATEGORY:** archaeology

**EXPLANATION:** In late September 2026, the Hebrew University of Jerusalem's Institute of Archaeology (excavation co-directors Prof. Uzi Leibner and Prof. Orit Peleg-Barkat, building on earlier Ophel excavations by Benjamin Mazar, Eilat Mazar, and Yosef Garfinkel) announced the discovery of a monumental Second Temple-period purification complex at the Ophel, immediately south of the Temple Mount in Jerusalem's Old City. The complex, covering roughly 280 square meters, comprises three large public mikva'ot (ritual immersion baths), a monumental plastered pool (about 12 by 9.5 meters, with steps descending from all four sides) dated to the early first century CE, a paved plaza, and an extensive water-supply and drainage system; one of the baths survives to a height of about 4.2 meters and retains a rare vaulted stone ceiling. Archaeologists interpret the scale and location — directly on the approach to the Temple — as evidence of organized, public ritual purification infrastructure serving pilgrims arriving for the three annual pilgrimage festivals in the final century before the Temple's destruction in 70 CE. This is independent physical corroboration of a Second Temple-era practice of communal ritual immersion tied to Temple access and pilgrimage, complementing (without duplicating) the project's existing entries on Second Temple purity practice (e.g., the Mount Scopus stone-vessel workshop and the Kiryat Gat mikveh, both different sites and different specific finds) and the Torah's own purity-law and pilgrimage-festival material.

**SOURCE:** "Ancient public mikveh complex, used for festival pilgrimage, uncovered at foot of Temple Mount," *The Times of Israel*, September 2026; corroborated by *The Jerusalem Post* ("Massive newly-unearthed complex may shed light on how Jewish pilgrims prepared to enter the Temple," article-909482), *Haaretz* ("Monumental Ancient Ritual Baths Unearthed at Foot of Temple Mount in Jerusalem," 24 Sept. 2026), and the excavators' own announcement via the Hebrew University/Armstrong Institute Ophel excavation project.
