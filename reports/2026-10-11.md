## Verified new items

TITLE: A Late Bronze Age Lachish Jar Inscription — The Root Š-L-Ṭ (as in Joseph's Title) Attested Centuries Before Its Supposed Persian-Period Origin
CATEGORY: linguistics
EXPLANATION: During the 2025 excavation season at Tel Lachish, excavators recovered a pottery sherd from the shoulder of a storage jar, found in a dump pit in Area FF and assigned to the site's final Late Bronze Age settlement horizon (Level VI, destroyed in the mid-12th century BCE). The sherd bears a short inscription in ink, in the standardized Linear Canaanite (cursive Proto-Canaanite) script, which the authors read as containing the personal name Bʿlšlṭ — built on the root š-l-ṭ ("to rule/have dominion"). This matters because biblical scholarship has widely treated the root š-l-ṭ in Biblical Hebrew as a late Aramaism borrowed into Hebrew only in the Persian period, based partly on the claim that the root is otherwise unattested before the 8th century BCE and that nearly all its biblical occurrences fall in books conventionally dated to the Persian period — with Genesis 41/42 (where Joseph is given the title "shalit," ruler of Egypt) and Psalm 119:133 noted by the authors themselves as awkward exceptions to that pattern. A genuine 12th-century-BCE Canaanite attestation of the same root, if the reading holds, would undercut the "late Aramaism" argument used by some critical scholars to date passages containing the word to the Persian period, and would instead fit a scenario in which the word was already current in Canaanite/early Hebrew usage long before then. This is one new, narrow epigraphic data point in an active linguistic-dating debate (addressed in the project's existing linguistics entries on Egyptian loanwords and anachronism), not a resolution of it — the inscription is partially broken, the reading of the name is the excavators' own proposal, and the article is too recent (just published) for the field to have weighed in with independent review.
SOURCE: D. Vainstub, I. Weissbein, H-G. Kang, S. Halevi & Y. Garfinkel, "A Late Bronze Age Canaanite Jar Inscription from the 2025 Excavation Season at Lachish," *Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology* 9 (2026), pp. 14–30.
URL: https://jjar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jjar/files/jjar09_02_lachish.pdf

TITLE: "Beit HaMikvaot" — A Monumental Second Temple-Period Ritual-Immersion Complex Uncovered on Jerusalem's Ophel
CATEGORY: archaeology
EXPLANATION: In September 2026, Hebrew University archaeologists Uzi Leibner and Orit Peleg-Barkat, excavating on the Ophel (just south of the Temple Mount, near the sealed Triple Gate), announced a roughly 280-square-meter Second Temple-period purification complex comprising three public mikvaot (ritual immersion pools) and a central pool about 12 by 9.5 meters with steps descending on all four sides; one vaulted bath survives to its full height of about 4.2 meters. Excavators also identified an earlier, smaller public purification complex beneath it, which they date to roughly the 1st century BCE. The excavators' own interpretation — reported consistently across multiple independent outlets — is that the complex's scale, design, and location near a main approach to the Temple Mount indicate it served Jewish pilgrims undergoing ritual purification before entering the Temple precinct, consistent with the purity practices Second Temple-period Jewish sources (rabbinic literature, Josephus, the New Testament) describe. This bears on the project's archaeology/history_corrob themes as physical confirmation of the scale of organized ritual-purification infrastructure around the Temple in the late Second Temple period; it does not itself corroborate any specific biblical verse, and as with any very recent excavation announcement, the interpretation of function (pilgrim purification versus another use) is the excavators' reading of the physical remains rather than an settled, independently peer-reviewed conclusion.
SOURCE: Reported by Haaretz, "Monumental Ancient Ritual Baths Unearthed at Foot of Temple Mount in Jerusalem," 24 September 2026; corroborated independently by i24NEWS, Ynetnews, Jewish Press, and the Armstrong Institute of Biblical Archaeology (excavation partner), all September 2026.
URL: https://www.haaretz.com/archaeology/2026-09-24/ty-article-magazine/monumental-ancient-ritual-baths-unearthed-at-foot-of-temple-mount-in-jerusalem/000001a0-cea3-d18d-afbf-ffe3d7810000
