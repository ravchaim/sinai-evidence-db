## Verified new item

TITLE: Early Attestation of the Root Š-L-Ṭ ("to Rule") in a Late Bronze Age Lachish Jar Inscription
CATEGORY: linguistics
EXPLANATION: During the 2025 excavation season at Tel Lachish, archaeologists recovered a ceramic jar fragment from a 12th-century BCE (Late Bronze Age) destruction context bearing six letters written in red ink on the shoulder. Published by Daniel Vainstub, Itamar Weissbein, Hoo Goo Kang, Shai Halevi, and Yosef Garfinkel as "A Late Bronze Age Canaanite Jar Inscription from the 2025 Excavation Season at Lachish" (*Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology* 9 [2026]: 14–30), the surviving letters — written in the standardized Linear Canaanite script — preserve the personal name Bʻlšlṭ ("Baal is ruler/lord"), built on the root š-l-ṭ ("to rule, have dominion"). The authors argue this pushes the attested use of this root in the Levant back to the Late Bronze Age, roughly five centuries earlier than the Persian-period Aramaic contexts in which it had generally been assumed to first appear in West Semitic. This is relevant to the project's linguistics theme because Genesis 41:43 gives Joseph the Egyptian-court title "shalit" (שליט), built on the same root — a title critics have cited as a linguistic anachronism on the theory that שליט is a late Aramaic loanword that could not have been used authentically in a Bronze Age Egyptian setting. Caveat: the peer-reviewed paper itself establishes only the earlier attestation date of the root š-l-ṭ in a Canaanite personal name; it does not itself discuss the Joseph narrative. The explicit connection to Genesis 41:43 is drawn in secondary, apologetics-oriented commentary (e.g., the Armstrong Institute for Biblical Archaeology), not by the paper's academic authors. The underlying epigraphic/linguistic dating claim is nonetheless a genuine, newly published, peer-reviewed finding, and it is fair to note that it weakens (without definitively refuting) the "late loanword" argument against the antiquity of Joseph's title — this remains a question of degree (how far back the root's broader semantic use in administrative/royal titulature specifically can be pushed) rather than a settled resolution.
SOURCE: Daniel Vainstub, Itamar Weissbein, Hoo Goo Kang, Shai Halevi, and Yosef Garfinkel, "A Late Bronze Age Canaanite Jar Inscription from the 2025 Excavation Season at Lachish," *Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology* 9 (2026): 14–30. Corroborated independently by the publication's listing on cris.huji.ac.il and by secondary coverage (Armstrong Institute for Biblical Archaeology; Biblical Archaeology Society).
