## Verified new item

TITLE: A Hieratic-Inscribed Jar at Tel Azekah — Egyptian Administration in Canaan at the Close of the Late Bronze Age
CATEGORY: archaeology
EXPLANATION: A storage jar excavated in 2019 by the Lautenschläger Azekah Expedition, from a destruction layer dated to the second half of the 12th century BCE, bears an ink hieratic (cursive Egyptian) inscription on its shoulder that the authors read as part of an administrative title, plausibly connected to tax collection. Petrographic analysis points to Tell eṣ-Ṣafi/Gath, roughly 8 km away, as the jar's likely origin (local production at Azekah is not fully excluded). The authors argue the find is among the latest securely dated hieratic texts from the southern Levant and indicates that Egyptian-trained scribal/administrative personnel were still active in the Shephelah at the very end of Egypt's New Kingdom imperial presence in Canaan. This bears on the project's "history_corrob"/archaeology themes in a modest, background way: it adds one more data point — alongside existing entries on Egyptian papyri, loanwords, and administrative texts touching Canaan — to the evidentiary picture of continued Egyptian cultural and administrative presence in the land during the Late Bronze–Iron Age transition, the period traditionally associated with Israel's early settlement. It does not corroborate any specific biblical verse or event, and should be read as general historical-background evidence, not as confirmation of a biblical claim.
SOURCE: D. Sweeney, S. J. Wimmer, O. Lipschits & S. Kleiman, "A Hieratic Inscribed Jar from the Late Bronze/Iron Age Transition at Tel Azekah," *Tel Aviv* 53(1), 2026, pp. 34–49 (published online 31 May 2026). Corroborated independently by press coverage in GreekReporter (June 2026) and La Brújula Verde (June 2026).
URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03344355.2026.2609320
