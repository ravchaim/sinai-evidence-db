One item survived independent verification, is not a duplicate of an existing entry, and is presented below with an important caveat about its actual age and contested status.

TITLE: The Uzziah-Era Wall — Radiocarbon Chronology Reattributes a City of David Fortification Ahead of Hezekiah's Reign
CATEGORY: archaeology
EXPLANATION: A peer-reviewed study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (Regev et al., "Radiocarbon chronology of Iron Age Jerusalem reveals calibration offsets and architectural developments," PNAS 121:19, published April 29, 2024), conducted by researchers from the Israel Antiquities Authority, Tel Aviv University, and the Weizmann Institute of Science, applied high-resolution radiocarbon "wiggle-matching" — over 100 dated organic samples (grape seeds, date pits, animal bones) cross-referenced against a European tree-ring calibration curve — to build a decade-level chronology of Iron Age Jerusalem construction phases that had previously relied on less precise ceramic typology. Among its findings, a defensive wall and fortification on the City of David's eastern slope, long attributed to King Hezekiah's late-8th-century BCE preparations against Assyrian siege, was instead dated roughly a century earlier, placing its construction in the reign of King Uzziah — consistent with the "great earthquake" referenced in Amos 1:1 and Zechariah 14:5, and with 2 Chronicles 26:9-10's account of Uzziah's building and fortification activity in Jerusalem. This specific finding has not previously appeared in this database. It is flagged here with two important caveats: (1) although the study is receiving renewed press coverage in 2026, the underlying peer-reviewed finding itself is from April 2024, not new research — it is being reported here because it is new to this database, not because it is recent; and (2) the reattribution is methodologically contested — archaeologist Israel Finkelstein has published a critique arguing that many of the radiocarbon samples came from fill and debris layers rather than sealed primary contexts, which he argues can produce an "old-dates effect" that pushes apparent construction dates earlier than the true occupation date, a caution directly relevant to how confidently the Uzziah/earthquake correlation should be treated.
SOURCE: J. Regev et al., "Radiocarbon chronology of Iron Age Jerusalem reveals calibration offsets and architectural developments," PNAS 121, no. 19 (April 29, 2024): e2321024121; critique: I. Finkelstein, "Observations on the article Regev et al., Radiocarbon Dates for Iron Age Jerusalem, PNAS 2024" (May 2024)
URL: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2321024121
