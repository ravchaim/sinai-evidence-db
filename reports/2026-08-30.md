TITLE: The Lachish "Bʻlšlṭ" Jar Inscription — Pushing Back the Root Behind Joseph's Title
CATEGORY: linguistics
EXPLANATION: A peer-reviewed article by Daniel Vainstub, Itamar Weissbein, Hoo-Goo Kang, Shai Halevi, and Yosef Garfinkel, "A Late Bronze Age Canaanite Jar Inscription from the 2025 Excavation Season at Lachish," published in 2026 in the Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology (vol. 9, pp. 14–30), reports a partially preserved six-letter inscription written in red ink on the shoulder of a ceramic jar, recovered during the 2025 season at Tel Lachish from Level VI — the site's final Late Bronze Age settlement horizon, destroyed in the mid-12th century BCE. The surviving letters, in a cursive form of the standardized Linear Canaanite script, allow the personal name Bʻlšlṭ ("Baal is ruler/Baal rules") to be read — a name built on the root š-l-ṭ ("to rule"). Hebrew-Bible scholarship has long treated this same root, as it appears in Biblical Hebrew (שלט, "ruler"), as an Aramaism confined almost entirely to Persian-period books, on the reasoning that the root is otherwise unattested outside Aramaic before the 8th century BCE — a dating argument for which Genesis 42:6 ("Joseph was the ruler [ha-shallit] over the land") and Psalm 119:133 were already flagged in the literature as awkward exceptions. This 12th-century BCE Canaanite attestation, roughly seven centuries earlier than the root's presumed Aramaic-loan window, is a genuine complication for that specific linguistic-dating argument, since it shows the root was current in the Canaanite milieu well before the Iron Age. This should not be overstated: it is one data point regarding one root, the reading rests on a broken and faded sherd (its recovery required multispectral imaging), and linguistic dating of Biblical Hebrew generally remains a contested and actively debated methodology among scholars (a point underscored by a March 2026 paper from Aaron Hornkohl of Cambridge defending the method's validity against recent challengers). It does not resolve the broader, unsettled question of when Genesis was composed or redacted — but it does remove one specific plank sometimes used to argue that the wording of Genesis 42:6 must reflect a late (Persian-period) linguistic layer.
SOURCE: Daniel Vainstub, Itamar Weissbein, Hoo-Goo Kang, Shai Halevi, and Yosef Garfinkel, "A Late Bronze Age Canaanite Jar Inscription from the 2025 Excavation Season at Lachish," Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology 9 (2026): 14–30
URL: https://jjar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jjar/files/jjar09_02_lachish.pdf
