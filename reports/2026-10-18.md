## New item

TITLE: The Hasmonean-Era Lower Aqueduct Section Near Ramat Rachel
CATEGORY: archaeology
EXPLANATION: On October 11, 2026, the Israel Antiquities Authority announced the exposure of a roughly 300-meter section of Jerusalem's "Lower Aqueduct" just south of Kibbutz Ramat Rachel, found during a salvage excavation (directed by Benyamin Storchan and Gil Freid) ahead of a planned residential neighborhood. The Lower Aqueduct itself is not a new discovery — it is a well-documented, gravity-fed channel running roughly 21 km from Solomon's Pools near Bethlehem to Jerusalem, previously exposed in sections as far back as 2019 and listed on UNESCO's tentative World Heritage list — but this is a newly excavated, unusually long and well-preserved stretch of it. The excavators attribute its original construction to the Hasmonean period (2nd–1st century BCE), supplying water toward the Temple Mount, with a Mamluk-era (plastered channel) and later an Ottoman-era (ceramic pipe) phase of reuse; multiple reports note the system remained in at least intermittent use until roughly a century ago. Because the aqueduct is Hasmonean rather than First Temple-period construction, and no specific Tanach verse is invoked by the excavators, this find bears on the project's archaeology layer as general corroboration of the monumental scale of Second Temple-era infrastructure built to supply Jerusalem and its Temple precinct — in the same vein as the database's existing entries on the Ophel mikvaot, the Pilgrimage Road, and the Kishle Hasmonean Wall — rather than as a direct textual corroboration. Sources differ slightly on wording for how long the system stayed in use (the IAA says "intermittent use until about 100 years ago"; one outlet's looser paraphrase says "nearly two millennia"), which is noted here for accuracy rather than treated as a discrepancy in the underlying find.
SOURCE: Israel Antiquities Authority announcement, October 11, 2026; reported independently by The Jerusalem Post, The Times of Israel/Israel National News, Algemeiner, VINnews, The Jewish Chronicle, and Israel365 News.
