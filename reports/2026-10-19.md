## New item

TITLE: Computational Word-Frequency Analysis Statistically Distinguishes Three Scribal Corpora in the Enneateuch
CATEGORY: linguistics
EXPLANATION: A 2025 peer-reviewed study — Faigenbaum-Golovin, Kipnis, Bühler, Piasetzky, Römer, and Finkelstein, "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship," *PLOS ONE* 20(6): e0322905 — applies a computational, word-frequency-based statistical method to 50 chapters across the Enneateuch (Genesis through Kings), pre-labeled by standard biblical-criticism categories into three corpora: the Deuteronomic layer (D), the Deuteronomistic History in Joshua–Kings (DtrH), and the Priestly source (P). Without assuming author identity in advance, the method distinguishes the three corpora from word-frequency signatures alone, and finds that D and DtrH pattern much more closely with each other than either does with P — a result the authors note is consistent with the long-standing scholarly consensus that the Priestly material is linguistically and stylistically distinct from the Deuteronomistic layers. This is directly relevant to the project's linguistics/literary themes because it bears on the contested question of single vs. multiple authorship of the Torah/Tanach's narrative-legal core: unlike the database's existing entries arguing for stylometric and literary *unity* (e.g., Radday and Shore's statistical study of Genesis, and Cassuto's literary-unity argument against source fragmentation), this newer computational study reaches the opposite conclusion — that distinguishable, statistically separable scribal traditions underlie these books. The authorship question (which corpora existed, when, and whether differences reflect distinct authors/schools versus a single author's stylistic variation across genres/purposes) remains an actively disputed question in biblical scholarship, and this study's framing and conclusions should be read as one data point within that ongoing debate, not as a settled resolution of it, and in tension with the unity-oriented arguments already catalogued elsewhere in this database.
SOURCE: Faigenbaum-Golovin, S., Kipnis, A., Bühler, A., Piasetzky, E., Römer, T., & Finkelstein, I. (2025). "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship." PLOS ONE, 20(6), e0322905.
URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0322905
