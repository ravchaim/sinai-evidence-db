TITLE: The Lachish Bʻlšlṭ Jar Inscription — An Early Attestation of the Root Behind Joseph's Title

CATEGORY: linguistics

EXPLANATION: During the 2025 excavation season at Tel Lachish, archaeologists recovered a fragmentary six-letter inscription written in red ink on the shoulder of a storage jar, from an unambiguous 12th-century BCE Late Bronze Age destruction context. Published by Daniel Vainstub, Itamar Weissbein, Hoo-Goo Kang, Shai Halevi, and Yosef Garfinkel in the *Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology* (vol. 9, 2026, pp. 14–30), the inscription is read as the personal name Bʻlšlṭ, written in the standardized Linear Canaanite script with cursive, ink-and-stylus features. The name is built on the root š-l-ṭ ("to rule, have authority"), a root that scholarship has generally treated as entering Hebrew only via a later Persian-period Aramaic loanword. The same root underlies the title Pharaoh gives Joseph in Genesis 41:40 — "shallit" (מֹשֵׁל). A 12th-century BCE attestation of the root in a Canaanite context pushes its presence in the region's linguistic environment several centuries earlier than the conventional loanword-dating assumption, which is relevant to arguments about whether vocabulary in the Joseph narrative is anachronistic for its claimed Late Bronze/early Iron Age setting. Important caveat: the paper's own contribution is the epigraphic and linguistic dating point (an early attestation of the š-l-ṭ root); the specific connection to Joseph's title in Genesis is an interpretive inference drawn by outside apologetics commentary (e.g., ArmstrongInstitute.org), not a claim made by the excavators/authors themselves, and should be presented as such rather than as the paper's own conclusion.

SOURCE: Vainstub, D., Weissbein, I., Kang, H-G., Halevi, S., and Garfinkel, Y., "A Late Bronze Age Canaanite Jar Inscription from the 2025 Excavation Season at Lachish," *Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology* 9 (2026): 14–30.

URL: https://jjar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jjar/files/jjar09_02_lachish.pdf

TITLE: A Peer-Reviewed Challenge to the Hittite-Treaty Structure Argument for Deuteronomy

CATEGORY: meta

EXPLANATION: The database already contains the well-known apologetic argument that Deuteronomy's structure (preamble, historical prologue, stipulations, document clause, witness list, blessings/curses) closely mirrors 14th–12th century BCE Hittite suzerain-vassal treaties, a form said to have fallen out of use after 1200 BCE — used to argue against a late, post-exilic composition of Deuteronomy. A 2026 peer-reviewed article by Mark Steven Francois, "Is It Time to Abandon the Hittite Treaty Structure of Deuteronomy?" (*Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 68.4: 705–726), directly challenges this parallel — notably from within evangelical biblical scholarship, not a source hostile to traditional authorship. Francois argues that, examined on their own terms, Deuteronomy's preamble and historical-prologue material show no positive textual evidence specifically tying them to Hittite treaty preambles, and questions whether the widely-cited Mendenhall/Kline-style structural correspondence is as tight as apologetics literature commonly presents it. This does not argue Deuteronomy is late or non-Mosaic; it argues that one specific supporting argument (the Hittite-treaty structural parallel) is contested even among scholars otherwise sympathetic to traditional dating, and should not be presented as an uncontested consensus. This is included as a corrective/caveat to the database's existing Hittite-treaty-parallel entries, in keeping with the project's commitment to flagging contested academic questions rather than presenting one side as settled.

SOURCE: Francois, M.S., "Is It Time to Abandon the Hittite Treaty Structure of Deuteronomy?" *Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society* 68.4 (2025): 705–726.

URL: https://etsjets.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/JETS_68.4_705_Francois.pdf
