## New item

TITLE: Hincks's Critical Philosophical Analysis of the Cosmological Fine-Tuning Argument for God
CATEGORY: philosophy
EXPLANATION: Adam D. Hincks (an astrophysicist and theologian at the University of Toronto's St. Michael's College) published a peer-reviewed article reviewing the standard "fine-tuning" argument — the observation that several physical constants and initial conditions of the universe fall within a narrow range that permits the existence of life — and critically tests the claim that God is the most probable explanation for this fine-tuning. Rather than treating the inference from fine-tuning to God as straightforward, Hincks argues it sits uneasily with the more apophatic ("God is radically unlike anything we can positively describe") strand of theology within the Abrahamic traditions, and instead offers his own metaphysical account of why fine-tuning is contingent, situating it within the classical philosophical "analogy of being" as a pointer toward the Divine rather than a direct proof. This is relevant to the project's philosophy layer, which already includes general theistic arguments (e.g., the Maimonidean First Cause argument, the argument from the universe's intelligibility), but the specific fine-tuning argument and its serious philosophical complications are not yet represented there. Because the paper's own conclusion is that the simple fine-tuning-implies-God inference is contested and philosophically complicated — not a settled proof — any entry drawn from it should present it as a nuanced, partly critical treatment of the argument, not as confirmation of design.
SOURCE: Hincks, A. D. (2026). "Does a Fine-Tuned Universe Tell Us Anything about God?" Zygon: Journal of Religion and Science, 60(4), 1145–1167. (Electronic publication 20 January 2026; also posted to arXiv as 2502.12083 in February 2025.)
URL: https://www.zygonjournal.org/article/id/16897/
