## New Item

**TITLE:** Psychophysical Harmony — A New Consciousness-Based Argument for Theism (Cutter & Crummett, 2025)

**CATEGORY:** philosophy

**EXPLANATION:** Brian Cutter (University of Notre Dame) and Dustin Crummett published "Psychophysical Harmony: A New Argument for Theism" in *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Religion*, vol. 11 (2025), pp. 33–71 (Oxford University Press). The paper identifies "psychophysical harmony" — the striking fact that phenomenal (subjective, conscious) states are correlated with physical states and with each other in ways that are functionally and rationally fortunate: for example, pain (rather than some neutral or pleasant sensation) is the phenomenal state correlated with tissue damage and avoidance behavior, and our verbal reports about our own conscious states are reliably made true by those very states. The authors argue this pattern of harmony is antecedently far more probable on theism (where a mind-designing God would have reason to coordinate the physical and the phenomenal) than on standard atheistic/naturalistic physicalism (where the coordination is a brute, inexplicably fortunate coincidence), and that this raises the probability of theism relative to its chief naturalistic rival. The authors are explicit that the argument is modest: it does not entail theism, does not purport to refute every non-theistic alternative, and is intentionally built to be independent of any prior refutation of physicalism and immune to a multiverse-style objection (unlike cosmological fine-tuning arguments, psychophysical correlations are not the kind of thing a multiverse of physical universes would help explain away). The argument has already generated active philosophical debate and critique since publication (e.g., a 2026 reply addressing a "brute fact" objection, and independent critical commentary disputing the inference), so it should be presented as a contested, actively-debated argument in professional philosophy of religion — a new, serious entry in the design/teleological-argument family relevant to the project's "philosophy" layer — rather than as a settled proof.

**SOURCE:** Brian Cutter & Dustin Crummett, "Psychophysical Harmony: A New Argument for Theism," *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Religion*, Vol. 11 (2025), pp. 33–71, Oxford University Press.
