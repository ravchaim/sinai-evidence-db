## New Item

**TITLE:** A Word-Frequency/AI Statistical Study Supporting Multiple Scribal Traditions in the Torah and Former Prophets (Faigenbaum-Golovin et al., 2025)

**CATEGORY:** literary

**EXPLANATION:** A peer-reviewed study — Faigenbaum-Golovin, Kipnis, Bühler, Piasetzky, Römer, and Finkelstein, "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship," *PLOS ONE* 20(6): e0322905 (published June 3, 2025; preprint on arXiv from October 2024) — applies a statistical word-frequency classification method to 49–50 chapters of the "Enneateuch" (the first nine books of the Hebrew Bible), pre-labeled by the authors according to standard academic source-critical categories: the old layer of Deuteronomy (D), the Deuteronomistic History in Joshua–Kings (DtrH), and the Priestly material (P). The method's automatic attributions coincided with these standard scholarly assessments in 84% of chapters (41 of 49), with a mean cross-validated accuracy of 85.8% (SD 5%) across 130 random train/test splits, and per-corpus true-positive rates of 78% (D), 72% (DtrH), and 95% (P). The study also found that D and DtrH cluster statistically closer to each other than either does to P — again matching the standard source-critical picture. Because the chapters were labeled by the authors according to the pre-existing Documentary Hypothesis/source-critical framework before the statistical test was run, the result is best described as showing that word-frequency signals are broadly consistent with, and can often reproduce, those traditional source divisions — not as an independent proof that the divisions are correct, nor as a claim about single vs. multiple human/divine authorship as such. Read fairly, it is a genuine, non-trivial complication for this project's literary-unity entries (e.g., the Cassuto literary-unity argument and the Radday–Shore stylometric-unity study of Genesis already in the database), which argue from stylistic/statistical evidence toward compositional unity; this 2025 study is a comparable statistical approach reaching the opposite directional conclusion for a different textual corpus (the wider Enneateuch rather than Genesis alone). The underlying authorship question — single author, unified redaction of older sources, or multiple independent scribal schools — remains a live, contested question in biblical scholarship, and this study should be read as one data point in that ongoing debate rather than as a settled verdict either way.

**SOURCE:** Shira Faigenbaum-Golovin, Alon Kipnis, Axel Bühler, Eli Piasetzky, Thomas Römer, Israel Finkelstein, "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship," *PLOS ONE* 20(6): e0322905 (2025), https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0322905; preprint: arXiv:2410.19883 (Oct. 2024).

**URL:** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0322905
