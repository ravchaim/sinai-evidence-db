## Verified new item

TITLE: Second Temple-Era Ritual Bath and Murex Dye Shell Uncovered at Shiloh
CATEGORY: archaeology
EXPLANATION: In late July 2026, Israel's Civil Administration Archaeology Unit, working with the Binyamin Regional Council, reported finding a Second Temple-period ritual bath (mikveh), a clay cooking pot, and a murex shell at the Shiloh National Heritage Site — the location Tanach identifies as the site of the Mishkan before the Ark moved to Jerusalem (Joshua 18:1; 1 Samuel 1–4). Some of the material came from sifting excavated soil at the "Treasures of Samaria" facility rather than from an in-situ excavation context, which limits the precision of any stratigraphic dating. Murex shells were the ancient source of tekhelet (purple-blue dye); rabbinic tradition ties this dye to tzitzit (Bamidbar 15:38) and to parts of the High Priest's garments (Shemos 28). The find is evidence of local dye-related activity at Shiloh persisting into the Second Temple period, centuries after the Tabernacle era represented by other entries already in this database (e.g., the Late Bronze Age cultic deposit at Shiloh). It should explicitly NOT be confused with a separate, more sensational claim reported the same week by a different excavation team (Associates for Biblical Research, led by Scott Stripling) working elsewhere at the site, who described a Late Bronze Age favissa (buried sacrificial-remains deposit) with gold and silver objects as possibly connected to the Tabernacle/Ark of the Covenant. That favissa claim (a) appears to be the same Late Bronze Age gold-jewelry find already represented in this database under "Shiloh's Late Bronze Age Cultic Deposit," and (b) is explicitly hedged by the excavators themselves, who state there is no definitive evidence the structure is the biblical Tabernacle — a claim other scholars, including Israeli archaeologists, have publicly disputed. Only the Second Temple ritual-bath/dye-shell finding (a distinct period, team, and location detail) is being proposed here as new.
SOURCE: Israel Civil Administration Archaeology Unit and Binyamin Regional Council, reported by Ynetnews and Israel National News, late July 2026
URL: https://www.ynetnews.com/jewish-world/article/sy9zd2wnze
