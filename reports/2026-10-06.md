## New Item

**TITLE:** A New Hebrew Ostracon from Lachish Bearing the Rare Biblical Name "Shaphan"

**CATEGORY:** archaeology

**EXPLANATION:** During the Fourth Expedition to Tel Lachish (a joint Hebrew University–Southern Adventist University project directed by Yosef Garfinkel, Michael Hasel, and Martin Klingbeil), excavators recovered in 2016, from the northern slope of the tell, the triangular lower-right corner of a small ceramic ostracon in very poor condition, stratigraphically assigned to Level II — the city destroyed by the Babylonians in 586 BCE. Because the ink had faded almost to invisibility, the team used reflectance imaging spectroscopy (hyperspectral imaging, 400–1,000 nm range) to recover the writing, and published the results in 2025 in the peer-reviewed Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology (Vainstub, Kang, Sober, Arad & Garfinkel). The authors "cautiously" reconstruct the surviving line as recording a date ("On the 1[6?] [day of the month]") followed by the personal name Shapan ("Shaphan") and the beginning of a patronymic ("[son of] ..."). The authors are explicit that this is a partial, cautious decipherment of a badly damaged ostracon, not a certain or complete reading. The name Shaphan is otherwise rare both in the Bible and in the epigraphic record; in the Bible it is best known as the name of the royal scribe/secretary of King Josiah who read the newly found Torah scroll to the king (2 Kings 22:3–10) and whose son Gemariah later sheltered Jeremiah's scribe Baruch (Jeremiah 36:10–12). The find does not identify this specific ostracon's "Shaphan" with the biblical scribe, or prove any connection between them — it is onomastic corroboration that the name was in genuine contemporary use in late-7th/early-6th-century BCE Judah, in the same Babylonian destruction horizon in which the biblical narrative places Judah's fall, which is the modest and correct way to present it. This complements, without duplicating, the project's existing entries on other First Temple-period bullae and seals bearing officials' names (e.g., the "Servant of Josiah" bulla, the Jehucal bulla, the City of David bullae naming officials in Jeremiah), none of which concern this specific ostracon or the name Shaphan.
