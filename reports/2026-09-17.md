TITLE: A 12th-Century BCE Lachish Jar Inscription and the Root Š-L-Ṭ
CATEGORY: linguistics
EXPLANATION: A jar inscription excavated at Tel Lachish during the 2025 season and published in 2026 (Vainstub, Weissbein, Kang, Halevi, and Garfinkel, "A Late Bronze Age Canaanite Jar Inscription from the 2025 Excavation Season at Lachish," Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology 9: 14–30) preserves six letters in red ink on a jar shoulder, recovered from a securely stratified Level VI context — Lachish's final Late Bronze Age settlement, destroyed in the mid-12th century BCE. The surviving letters read the personal name Bʻlšlṭ, built on the root š-l-ṭ ("to rule"), a root that had "hitherto been widely considered a much later (Persian-period) loan from Aramaic" into Canaanite/Hebrew, per the authors' own framing as reported in secondary coverage of the paper. This inscription pushes the attested use of that root in a Canaanite personal name back several centuries earlier than the Persian period, which bears on this project's linguistics theme insofar as arguments for a late (post-exilic) composition date of biblical passages sometimes lean on the presence of roots like š-l-ṭ (the same root underlies "ha-shallit," Joseph's title in Genesis 42:6) as supposed late Aramaisms. Two caveats are important: first, the direct primary-source PDF (hosted at jjar.huji.ac.il) and the paper's own academia.edu listing were both blocked by this session's network egress filtering, so this entry relies on the paper's bibliographic details (real, cross-confirmed via multiple independent searches and its indexing at a genuine Hebrew University-affiliated journal) plus consistent secondary summaries of its stated conclusion — not a firsthand reading of the full article text. Second, the broader question of how much weight any single loanword or "Aramaism" can bear for dating a biblical text is itself a live, contested area within Hebrew Bible linguistics (scholars disagree on whether Aramaic-Hebrew contact in any given period reliably signals a text's date), so this finding should be read as complicating one specific late-dating argument about this particular root, not as settling the linguistic-dating debate generally.
SOURCE: Daniel Vainstub, Itamar Weissbein, Hoo-Goo Kang, Shai Halevi, and Yosef Garfinkel, "A Late Bronze Age Canaanite Jar Inscription from the 2025 Excavation Season at Lachish," Jerusalem Journal of Archaeology 9 (2026): 14–30
URL: https://jjar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jjar/files/jjar09_02_lachish.pdf
