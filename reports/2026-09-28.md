## New Item

**TITLE:** Cosmological Fine-Tuning — The 2026 Three-Way Philosophical Reassessment (Goff, Lewis & Barnes)

**CATEGORY:** philosophy

**EXPLANATION:** A 2026 peer-reviewed paper, "Cosmological fine-tuning: the view from 2025," by philosopher Philip Goff (Durham University) and physicists Geraint Lewis and Luke Barnes, published in *Religious Studies* (Cambridge University Press), surveys the current physical evidence for cosmological fine-tuning — the observation that small changes to fundamental constants (the authors highlight the cosmological constant's value, whose predicted and observed magnitudes differ by a factor of roughly 10^120) would render the universe lifeless — and evaluates three live explanatory frameworks: traditional theism, the multiverse hypothesis, and a "pantheistic" God of limited power that is identified with the universe itself. Notably, the authors do not uniformly endorse classical theism (Goff is independently known as a proponent of panpsychism/cosmopsychism and treats limited pantheism as a serious contender); the paper's relevance to this project is narrower and more modest than "physicists prove God" framing would suggest: it is a 2026 acknowledgment, from working physicists and a philosopher, that the fine-tuning phenomenon is real and unresolved, that naturalistic explanations (principally the multiverse) face genuine philosophical objections (e.g., the "inverse gambler's fallacy"/understated-evidence critique raised against multiverse reasoning), and that theism remains, in the authors' own framing, "enormously boosted" relative to naturalism if it is even somewhat plausible that a God would want to create a universe supporting embodied life. This is best used as an up-to-date, contested academic touchpoint for the philosophy-of-cosmology argument already represented in the database, not as new physical evidence — and it should be presented honestly as three-way undecided among the paper's own authors, not as a theism-only conclusion.

**SOURCE:** Philip Goff, Geraint Lewis, and Luke Barnes, "Cosmological fine-tuning: the view from 2025," *Religious Studies* 62, Special Issue S1 (Cambridge University Press, first published online 8 October 2025, print 2026): S100–S110.

**URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/religious-studies/article/cosmological-finetuning-the-view-from-2025/E134326EB1A48C040F593BDAC266AFC2
