## New Item

**TITLE:** Two New Y-Chromosome Lineages with Likely Silk Road Origins in Kurdish and Bukharan Jewry (Avotaynu DNA Project, 2026)

**CATEGORY:** genetics

**EXPLANATION:** The Avotaynu DNA Project — a citizen-science/genealogy Y-chromosome study (administered by the nonprofit Avotaynu Research Partnership, using FamilyTreeDNA "Big Y" sequencing) that has deliberately focused on sampling non-Ashkenazi Jewish communities (Sephardi, Mizrahi, Kurdish, Bukharan, Maghrebi, Romaniote, and others) — announced preliminary findings on August 17, 2026 in a post titled "Two Newly Identified Y-Chromosome Lineages with likely Silk Road origins in Kurdish and Bukharan Jewry: Preliminary Findings." The announcement describes a previously undescribed Y-chromosome lineage, designated Avotaynu AB-370 (haplogroup J-FTF9916), found among men of Kurdish Jewish descent (with a carrier traced to Iraqi Kurdistan) and also detected in a man of Kaifeng Jewish (Chinese) descent; the most recent common male ancestor of these geographically distant carriers is estimated at approximately 550 CE (95% confidence interval: 269 BCE–1122 CE). The project interprets the shared lineage as a genetic trace of historical Jewish movement along Silk Road trade networks connecting Kurdistan, Bukhara, and China. This bears on the genetics layer's broader theme of tracing diaspora Jewish communities' shared paternal ancestry and historical interconnection, complementing — without duplicating — existing entries on the Cohen Modal Haplotype, the Lemba tribe, and genome-wide shared Middle Eastern ancestry, none of which address this specific Kurdish/Bukharan/Kaifeng lineage or Silk Road migration signal.

**Verification caveat (read before using this entry):** This is a non-peer-reviewed, preliminary finding from a genealogy/citizen-science DNA project, not an academic journal publication, and should be labeled as such if added to the database.
