## Notes on leads investigated and excluded (for transparency)

- **Computational test of the Priestly source (Bühler, Yoffe, Dershowitz, Piasetzky, Finkelstein, Römer & Sober, "Exploring the Stylistic Uniqueness of the Priestly Source in Genesis and Exodus Through a Statistical/Computational Lens," *Zeitschrift für die alttestamentliche Wissenschaft* 136(2), 2024, pp. 165–190, DOI 10.1515/zaw-2024-2001)** — Confirmed via multiple independent search results (DeGruyter journal listing, ResearchGate, a related 2023 ACL *Findings* preprint by largely the same team, and a corrigendum in ZAW 136(3)) that this paper genuinely exists and applies a statistical/computational method to test whether the Priestly (P) material in Genesis and Exodus is linguistically distinguishable from non-P material — a direct, modern empirical engagement with the Documentary Hypothesis, and thus a counter-consideration to this project's literary-unity entries (e.g. the Cassuto and Radday/Shore items already in `data.json`). However: (a) it is from 2024, predating this project's daily-search window by well over a year, so it is not "new" in the relevant sense and may well have already been considered and passed over in an earlier session; (b) secondary summaries gave conflicting pictures of how strong the result actually is — one source described "statistically significant" differences, while another reported that the more conservative significance test used in the paper (a cyclic-shift test) produced p-values of 0.08 (Genesis) and 0.06 (Exodus), both above the conventional 0.05 threshold; (c) with `WebFetch` down today, I could not open the primary PDF or the DeGruyter page myself to confirm which characterization is accurate. Per this project's verification standard, an item whose actual conclusion I cannot pin down from the primary source is excluded rather than guessed at. Flagged here only as a lead for a future session (with working page-fetch access) to verify directly against the paper's own text and results tables before drafting — note for the human reviewer that, if confirmed, this is a case where the "evidence" cuts against the database's general thesis on literary unity and should be represented honestly as a contested, borderline-significant statistical result, not a settled refutation or confirmation of anything.
- **Figurine-mold discovery at the Kishle/Tower of David (IAA, announced July 27, 2026)** — A genuine, well-covered find: the first known production mold for a First Temple-period (8th–6th century BCE) female-face figurine found in Jerusalem itself, suggesting local manufacture. Scholars quoted in coverage note the figurines' purpose/identification (fertility goddess, Asherah, or something else) remains debated and is not settled by this find. Topically, this is the same idolatry-evidence theme already covered by the existing entry "The Judean Pillar Figurine Mold — Physical Evidence of the Idolatry the Prophets Condemned." Treated as a duplicate topic and excluded, per the project's "err toward already covered" rule.
- **Physical re-examination of the Sassoon Codex with multispectral/UV imaging (Jewish Link, July 2026)** — A real, ongoing scholarly examination (the first physical study of the manuscript since its 1989 purchase) recovering previously illegible text and comparing it against the Aleppo Codex. This concerns the same manuscript already covered by the existing entry "Codex Sassoon 1053 — The Oldest Nearly Complete Hebrew Bible." Treated as a citation-upgrade candidate for that existing entry rather than a new topic, and excluded here.
- **Great Isaiah Scroll "originally two separate scrolls" argument (Fidanzio, in *The Great Isaiah Scroll: A Voice From the Desert*, reported by Times of Israel, January 2026)** — A genuine scholarly argument, building on a 2021 PLOS ONE pattern-recognition study (Popović/Dhali/Schomaker) that had already identified two scribal hands with a break at columns 27–29. Fidanzio's addition is a physical/manufacture-based argument that the two halves were originally separate scrolls later joined. This is a real but incremental refinement of a textual-criticism point about one specific scroll's internal composition; it does not provide an independent new line of evidence bearing on Torah historicity, authenticity, or transmission in the sense this project tracks (the existing textual-stability entries already cover Qumran manuscript evidence generally), so it was excluded as not clearly distinct from existing coverage.
- **Shabbat-observance mental-health research (OneTable/Research Square preprint on ~1,019 young adults; a 2025 ISA conference paper surveying 331 observant Jews)** — Both are real but weak: one is an unpublished preprint the authors themselves call exploratory, the other a conference paper, not a peer-reviewed journal article. Per the project's preference for peer-reviewed sources and its caution against overclaiming from thin data, neither clears the bar for inclusion.
- **Covenant Code literary-dependence-on-Hammurabi theory (a recent monograph proposing a Neo-Assyrian-period, 740–640 BCE, direct literary borrowing)** — A real line of argument, but I could not independently confirm the author, exact title, or publication venue/date from search results, so it was excluded as an unverified citation.
