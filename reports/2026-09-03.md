TITLE: The Fine-Tuning Argument — A 2026 Physicist-Philosopher Survey of the Evidence and Its Explanations
CATEGORY: philosophy
EXPLANATION: A 2026 review article by physicist Geraint F. Lewis, physicist Luke A. Barnes, and philosopher Philip Goff, published in the peer-reviewed journal *Religious Studies* (Cambridge University Press), surveys the current scientific evidence that the fundamental physical constants and initial conditions of the universe appear improbably well-suited for the existence of complex life — the "fine-tuning" observation that has become widely acknowledged in physics even among researchers who reject a theistic explanation. The authors then examine three competing explanatory frameworks for this fine-tuning: traditional theism, the multiverse hypothesis, and a pantheistic "God of limited power." Notably, the article is a balanced survey rather than a brief for any one position — the authors present theism as one live explanatory option among several, not as the demonstrated conclusion. This is a genuinely new (2026) peer-reviewed contribution to the classic fine-tuning design argument that is not yet represented in the database; existing cosmology-layer entries address specific scriptural/scientific concordances (e.g., Big Bang, embryology, sanitation laws) but none currently engage the fine-tuning-of-physical-constants argument itself. Because the source article explicitly treats theism as one of several live hypotheses rather than the proven explanation, any database entry drawing on it should present it as a contested philosophical argument under active scholarly discussion, not as settled science pointing to a Creator.
SOURCE: Lewis, G., Barnes, L., & Goff, P. (2026). "Cosmological fine-tuning: the view from 2025." *Religious Studies*, 62, S100–S110. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412525101170
