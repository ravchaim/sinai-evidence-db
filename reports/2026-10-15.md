## New Item

TITLE: Computational Word-Frequency Study Statistically Distinguishes Priestly from Deuteronomic/Deuteronomistic Biblical Hebrew
CATEGORY: literary
EXPLANATION: A June 2025 peer-reviewed study in *PLOS ONE* — "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship" by Shira Faigenbaum-Golovin, Alon Kipnis, Axel Bühler, Eli Piasetzky, Thomas Römer, and Israel Finkelstein — applied a statistical method sensitive to deviations in word (and n-gram) frequencies to 50 chapters pre-labeled, on standard source-critical grounds, into three corpora: the older layer of Deuteronomy (D), the Deuteronomistic History running from Joshua through Kings (DtrH), and the Priestly source material in the Torah (P). The model found the Priestly material to be the most linguistically distinctive of the three, while Deuteronomy and the Deuteronomistic History overlapped substantially on several linguistic features; a small number of chapters were classified by the model as a better statistical fit for a corpus other than the one assigned to them by standard scholarship, which the authors attribute partly to limited sample size. This is directly relevant to the project's literary layer, but it cuts in the opposite direction from entries already in the database that argue for compositional and stylistic unity across the Torah (e.g., Cassuto's literary-unity argument and the Radday–Shore stylometric study of Genesis): it lends quantitative, statistically-grounded support to the mainstream source-critical distinction between Priestly and non-Priestly material, rather than against it. The authors themselves are explicit that this does not mean the text has "authors" in a modern sense — co-author Thomas Römer told The Times of Israel the texts were reworked by redactors over time — and the paper should be read as one data point in an active, unresolved academic debate about biblical composition (a debate scholars such as Hornkohl, Rezetko, Young, and Naaijer are still actively contesting on linguistic-dating grounds generally), not as a settled verdict either way. Flagging this explicitly as counter-leaning evidence for the project's literary-unity entries, per the project's standing instruction to include genuine counter-evidence rather than omit it.
SOURCE: Faigenbaum-Golovin, S., Kipnis, A., Bühler, A., Piasetzky, E., Römer, T., & Finkelstein, I. (2025). "Critical biblical studies via word frequency analysis: Unveiling text authorship." *PLOS ONE*, 20(6), e0322905.
URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0322905
