## New item

TITLE: The Kiryat Gat Mikveh — A Previously Unknown Second Temple-Era Jewish Settlement
CATEGORY: archaeology
EXPLANATION: In September 2026 the Israel Antiquities Authority announced the extraction of a fully intact, 25-ton, Second Temple-period stepped ritual bath (mikveh) from a construction site in the Carmei Gat neighborhood of Kiryat Gat, in the seam between the Judean Lowlands and the northern Negev. The excavation directors (Elena Kogan-Zahavi, Maayan Margulis, and Shira Lifshitz) stated that this is the first and only Jewish settlement known in that specific geographic seam — no such settlement had previously been documented there — and that it appears to have been abandoned around the time of the Bar Kokhba Revolt (2nd century CE). Because a stepped, plastered immersion pool of this type is a specifically Jewish installation tied to Torah-mandated ritual purity practice (tohorat ha-mishpacha, niddah, and related laws), its discovery is diagnostic of a previously unrecorded Jewish population center, extending the known geographic footprint of Second Temple-period Jewish settlement and of everyday halachic practice beyond what the archaeological record had shown. This is a fresh data point (not a restatement of an already-catalogued mikveh) bearing on the project's archaeological and sociological themes: it is physical evidence of widespread, geographically dispersed adherence to Torah purity law among ordinary Jewish communities, not just in major known centers like Jerusalem.
SOURCE: Israel Antiquities Authority announcement, September 2026; reported independently by The Times of Israel, Haaretz, The Jewish Chronicle, JNS, All Israel News, Israel365 News, and VINnews, all naming the same excavation directors and dig details.
URL: https://www.timesofisrael.com/25-ton-second-temple-era-mikveh-lifted-from-ground-intact-during-kiryat-gat-construction/
