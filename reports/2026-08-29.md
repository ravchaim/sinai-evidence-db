TITLE: Nebuchadnezzar II's Own Building Inscriptions — Two New Cylinders from the Kish Ziggurat
CATEGORY: history_corrob
EXPLANATION: A peer-reviewed article by Ahmed Ali Jawad et al., "Two Inscribed Cylinders of Nebuchadnezzar II from the Ziggurat of Kish," published in the journal *Iraq* (Cambridge University Press, for the British Institute for the Study of Iraq) in late 2025, presents the first foundation-text confirmation of Nebuchadnezzar II's restoration of the ziggurat of the god Zababa at ancient Kish (Tell al-Uhaimir, Iraq). The two clay cylinders, inscribed in Neo-Babylonian cuneiform and written in the first person, were found by local residents in 2013 and handed to Iraq's State Board of Antiquities and Heritage; publication of a scholarly edition and translation followed over a decade later. The text is a standard royal building/dedication inscription in which Nebuchadnezzar II (r. 605–562 BCE) — the same king who besieged and destroyed Jerusalem, and who is already represented in this database via the Babylonian Chronicle BM 21946 and Jehoiachin's ration tablets — boasts of his temple-restoration work, matching the self-glorifying "great Babylon that I have built" building-king persona attributed to him in Daniel 4:30. This finding does not mention Judah, Jerusalem, or any biblical event, so it should not be overstated as evidence for a specific biblical narrative; its evidentiary value is narrower and more indirect: it is one more primary, contemporaneous document in Nebuchadnezzar's own words corroborating the historical reality of the kind of large-scale, personally-inscribed royal building program that the biblical portrait of him presupposes.
SOURCE: Ahmed Ali Jawad et al., "Two Inscribed Cylinders of Nebuchadnezzar II from the Ziggurat of Kish," Iraq (Cambridge University Press / British Institute for the Study of Iraq, 2025)
